# Dune Rider: build brief

Written 2026-11-15. This is the working brief for taking Dune Rider from a playable first pass to a realistic sand-dune dirt bike simulator. Sections 6 and 7 are filled in from simulation runs, not from guesswork.

## 1. Goal

A dirt bike simulator on procedurally generated sand dunes that looks and feels real:

1. **Front POV based on a real dirt bike.** You sit behind the bars of a motocross bike and see what a rider sees: bars, levers, hands, front fender, the fork working, the horizon tilting as you lean.
2. **Real daytime.** The sun moves. The game starts at your actual local time of day and runs through sunrise, noon, golden hour, dusk and night, with the light, sky colour and shadows changing to match.
3. **More realism in the ride.** The bike and the sand behave like the real thing: spray, ruts, weight shifts, a rider who moves.
4. **High-fidelity graphics.** Close to photographic sand, sky and light.

## 2. Decisions and assumptions

| Decision | Choice | Why | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assets vs code | Everything stays generated by code (confirmed by Matthew 2026-11-15: "Model in code") | Keeps the one-file build; sand and sky do not need assets | Confirmed. A slot for a real model can still be added later without redoing the rest. |
| "Real daytime events" | A full 24 h sun cycle, starting at the player's local clock time | Closest reading of the request | Assumed. Weather is out of scope unless asked. |
| Reference bike | 450 cc motocross bike: 1.48 m wheelbase, 21 in front / 19 in rear wheel, 300 mm suspension travel, ~110 kg bike + ~85 kg rider | Standard dimensions for the class | Set |
| Platform | Desktop browser with WebGPU, keyboard or gamepad | Same as the base project | Set |
| Frame rate target | 60 fps at 1440p on Apple silicon or a mid-range GPU | Smooth riding matters more than any single effect | Set |
| Sun path | Rises and sets along one fixed compass line, passing overhead at noon | Lets every dune shadow at every hour be looked up from two baked maps instead of re-baked (see 5.2) | Set, trade-off noted in 8 |

## 3. Where it stands (start of this brief)

Working: endless 2 km tile of dunes at 1 m resolution, quadtree detail levels to ~7 km, baked sky and dune shadows for one fixed late-afternoon sun, live bike shadow, ripples, sparkle, haze, 240 Hz two-wheel physics, tyre tracks, dust, synthesized engine and wind, chase camera, menu and HUD. Production file 41 KB.

Not there yet: fixed sun, chase camera only, stylised bike and rider, rider is a statue, no spray, tracks are painted on rather than pressed in, no bloom or motion blur.

## 4. Phases

Each phase ends playable. Acceptance criteria are what gets checked before moving on.

### Phase 1: Front POV
- Camera at the rider's eyes; `C` switches between POV and chase.
- Head behaviour: follows the bike's heading, rolls with part of the lean, stays more level than the chassis over bumps, dips on landings.
- Cockpit you can see: bars with crossbar pad, grips, brake and clutch levers, handguards, front number plate, fender, fork legs that visibly compress.
- Hands stay on the grips when the bars turn (forearms follow).
- The rider's own helmet is not drawn in POV but still casts a shadow.
- **Accept:** no geometry clipping through the camera at full lock, full compression or in a crash; horizon roll matches lean direction; hands attached at full lock both ways.

### Phase 2: Time of day
- Sun moves along its path; sky is recomputed live on the GPU from the same atmosphere model.
- Dune shadows correct at every hour from baked horizon maps.
- Sunlight colour, sky light and exposure follow the sun. Stars and a dim moonlit ambient at night; headlight on the bike.
- Starts at local clock time. `T` changes how fast time runs (paused, real time, 1 h per minute, 1 h per 10 s). `[` and `]` step an hour. Clock on the HUD.
- **Accept:** shadows point away from the sun at 07:00, 12:00 and 17:30; no popping when time runs fast; night is dark but rideable with the headlight; load time not noticeably longer.

### Phase 3: Ride realism
- Roost: sand thrown off the rear tyre in an arc, heavier with wheelspin.
- Tracks pressed into the sand (shaded as grooves from the sun's direction), not just darkened.
- Rider moves: stands on rough ground, sits and leans in turns, weight back on drops, absorbs landings.
- Sand blowing off crests in the wind.
- Engine sound with gear changes and load; landing thumps; sand hiss.
- Tuning pass on grip, slides and jump behaviour against the numbers in 6.
- **Accept:** stress simulation shows no stuck states and a crash rate that comes from rider error, not from the terrain.

### Phase 4: High-fidelity graphics
- HDR rendering with bloom, sun glare, subtle motion blur, heat shimmer near the ground at midday.
- Finer sand up close: real surface relief on ripples, self-shadowing at low sun.
- Soft dune shadow edges, contact shadows under the bike.
- Rocks and dry scrub in the troughs for scale.
- Bike pass: proper tyre tread, spokes, chain, radiator, exhaust, decals and number graphics; cloth-like rider gear.
- **Accept:** side-by-side with reference photos of dunes at three times of day; 60 fps at 1440p.

### Phase 5 (optional, needs the asset decision)
- Real bike and rider model with textures, loaded from files beside `index.html`.

## 5. Technical design notes

### 5.1 POV camera
Eye point in bike coordinates sits at the front of the helmet. View direction is built from the bike's yaw, half of its pitch and about 60% of its lean, plus a small look-into-the-turn offset from yaw rate. Near plane drops to 8 cm so forearms do not clip. The helmet becomes its own mesh part so it can be skipped in the main pass.

Forearms become two dynamic parts: a unit tube stretched each frame from the fixed elbow to wherever the grip is after steering.

### 5.2 Moving sun without re-baking shadows
For a sun that travels in one vertical plane, a point on the ground is in shadow exactly when the terrain's skyline in the sun's compass direction is higher than the sun. So bake, once at load, the skyline height (as a slope) toward the sunrise side and toward the sunset side for every metre of ground. At any time of day the shader compares the sun's elevation with the stored value. Cost at runtime: one texture read that is already happening.

### 5.3 Live sky
The atmosphere model moves from a one-off CPU bake to a small GPU pass (256 x 128) run whenever the sun has moved. Sun colour and total sky light are computed on the CPU from the same model with a coarse sample set. Exposure follows the brightness of sunlit sand, with a ceiling so night stays dark.

## 7b. The original's look, studied and ported (2026-11-15)

Read from the Abyssal Garden source in git history (`main:source/src/shaders.js`, `main.js`). What gives it its quality, and what was done with each part here:

| Their technique | How it works there | Here |
|---|---|---|
| High-range rendering | Scene drawn in linear light to an `rgba16float` target, clamped at 40; nothing is tone-mapped until the last pass | **Ported.** Every scene shader now outputs linear light |
| Bloom | First downsample weights each tap by 1 / (1 + brightest channel) and clamps at 24 so single glints cannot flicker; 6 levels down, then added back up; mixed in at about 6% plus 3% additive | **Ported** as written |
| Light shafts | Half resolution, jittered march along each view ray testing the shadow map, with extinction; linear depth kept in alpha; 3 x 3 depth-aware blur; depth-aware upsample in the composite | **Ported and adapted.** The march tests the baked skyline (how high the dune shadow reaches above the ground at each point) instead of a shadow map, so the haze is cut by dune shadows at any hour |
| Composite and grade | Exposure, vignette, ACES curve, an S-curve on luminance, highlights eased toward white, slight desaturation with a warm lift, gamma, two-sample dither | **Ported**, with the vignette and contrast eased for a bright desert |
| Baked openness | A volume storing how open each spot is and how much skylight reaches it, used to darken ambient light | **Ported as a 2D map**: sky openness from the skyline in 8 directions, darkening the troughs |
| Density | Millions of instances in culling cells with distance tiers | **Started.** About 8,900 stones and 600 dry bushes gathered in the sheltered low ground, each with a shadow that lengthens as the sun drops. No collision yet |
| Frame-rate governor | Measures frame time each second and scales the pixel budget, then draw distance | **Ported, simplified** (pixel budget only) |
| Baked detail texture | Tileable slope, height and mottling with mip levels | **Not ported.** Ripples are still computed per pixel; this is the next step for close-up sand |
| Composed viewpoints | World dressed around six camera positions | Not applicable to an endless field; revisit as "points of interest" |
| Visual critic loop | A separate agent sees only renders and lists defects until none are above minor | **Adopted.** See below |

Measured after the port: 76 fps at 1034 x 1082 with the full chain, page ready in 1.4 s, production file 58 KB.

## 7f. Deep study of the original: everything else that makes it look the way it does

Second pass through `main:source/src` (shaders, renderer, world generator), covering what the first pass (7b) did not. "Here" says where Dune Rider stands on each.

| # | Their technique | How it works there | Here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Baked detail texture** | One 1024 x 1024 tileable texture baked at load: two channels of slope, one of height, one of slow mottling, from warped noise, ridges, cracks and pits; full mip chain; 8x anisotropic. Sampled on three planes at **four scales at once** (0.1, 0.43, 1.9 and 6.7 per metre), plus two very large taps of the mottling for colour drift | **Missing.** Our ripples and grain are computed per pixel at one or two scales. | 3 | **Distance-faded detail octaves** | Each normal octave fades out before it gets finer than the pixels (the finest by 10 m, the next by 60 m) | Have the idea (ripple fade by pixel footprint), not the octaves |
| 4 | **Strata and patches in albedo** | Rock colour comes from banded beds picked by a hash, darkened seams, encrustation and algae masks | Sand has drift, streaks and trough darkening; rocks are one noise |
| 5 | **Reflected ambient and a colour boost** | Ambient light is also sampled along the mirror direction with a grazing-angle weight; lit colour gets extra saturation in proportion to incoming light | Bike has the reflection; sand and rocks do not |
| 6 | **Two-tap occlusion from the openness volume** | Openness sampled 1.5 m and 4.5 m out along the normal, giving tight and broad occlusion together | Have a single 2D openness value for the dunes; nothing for the ground under rocks |
| 7 | **Sun-projected animated texture** | A tiling pattern redrawn every frame, projected along the sun, three taps for colour fringing, faded to its mean at distance so it never shimmers; gated so it never shows on unlit faces | Missing. | 8 | **Plants as real geometry** | No alpha textures: every blade is a strip of triangles so anti-aliasing is clean. Light passes through (back-light plus a forward-scatter lobe). | 9 | **Particles that are lit** | Each sprite takes shadow, sun and ambient per vertex, so motes brighten inside light shafts. | 10 | **Glint sprites** | Separate tiny additive sprites with a twinkle and a soft falloff, size-limited in pixels | Sand sparkle is done in the surface shader, near the camera only |

### Port order (most visual gain first)

1. Baked sand and rock detail texture with four-scale sampling and macro colour taps (#1, with #2 and #3). Fixes flat, tiled-looking sand at every distance and gives rocks real surface.
2. Sand colour: darker albedo, beds and patches, reflected sky on crests, saturation with light (#4, #5, #14). Fixes washed-out midday.
3. Lit particles with the pixel floor, air motes that catch the shafts, glint sprites (#9, #10).
4. Drifting sand sheets and cloud shadows from a sun-projected animated texture; heat shimmer in post (#7).
5. Scrub as back-lit grass blades with no-pop tiers; then density through cell-sorted instancing: pebbles, tufts (#8, #12).
6. Occlusion under rocks and two-scale openness (#6); softer, larger bike shadow (#11).
7. Horizon blend (#15) and view-driven exposure (#16).

## 7i. Fold-out wings (2026-11-15, Matthew's request)

`F` folds the wings out or away. Three hinged sections a side stow flat beside the rear wheel; on deploy the root section swings out, twists level, and the middle and tip sections open in turn, while two tail fins swing up off the rear fender. Built from the bike's own vocabulary: brushed-metal skin with panel joins and rivets over frame-tube ribs, an exhaust-pipe leading edge, fender-plastic blades on the trailing edge, hinge barrels, white winglets.

Flight model: lift and drag from speed and angle of attack over 5.5 m2 of wing, a stall past about 18 degrees, lift tilted with the bank. In the air with wings out, brake pulls the nose up (flare), throttle pushes it over (dive), steering banks, and the bike weathervanes into its flight path. Wings fold on a crash.

## 7m. Handling, the wings and booster as one system, and pans (2026-11-16)

Asked for: better motorbike control, thought given to how the wings and booster relate, and more flat areas to build speed. Measured first with `node handling.mjs`, then changed, then measured again.

**Handling.** The baseline showed why the bike felt vague: with the throttle held (always, on a keyboard) the front wheel carried so little weight that it could only corner at about 0.5 g, and steering was a fixed angle far beyond what the tyres could use, so the front just ploughed.

| Full steering, throttle held | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Cornering at 54 km/h entry | 0.54 g, 175 m radius | 0.77 g, 84 m radius |
| Cornering at 108 km/h entry | 0.59 g, 217 m radius | 0.80 g, 125 m radius |
| Time to straighten after letting go | 0.51 s | 0.06 to 0.08 s |
| Sideways move, 0.4 s right then 0.4 s left at 72 km/h | 0.6 m | 1.1 m |

What changed in `physics.js`:
- Full steering asks for the tightest turn the tyres can hold at the current speed (`lock`), not a fixed angle.
- In a turn the rider eases the throttle by up to 40% and slides forward, which puts weight back on the front tyre.
- He steers against any turning that was not asked for. This catches slides; an earlier version that reacted to slide angle left the bike drifting for over a second at 55 to 70 km/h.
- Steering returns to centre faster than it goes on.
- In the air, steering whips the bike three times harder, and hands off it swings round to face its direction of travel before landing.
- Braking from 108 km/h takes 55 m. 0 to 100 km/h takes 4.9 s. Both unchanged.

**Wings and booster.** They were two separate gadgets. Now:
- The booster breathes rammed air: thrust is 2600 N at rest, rising 40 N per m/s to a ceiling of 5200 N. Speed makes a burn worth more, whether the speed came from a flat or from a stoop.
- The burn is one resource. It recharges only after 3 s on the ground (was 0.6 s, which allowed near-continuous boosting on the ground). Gliding never recharges it.
- The wings stop pulling at about 3.2 g, so a fast pull-out is a long arc and not a wall of drag.
- On the ground with the wings out, a burn lifts the nose once there is flying speed: a flat is a runway. Without a burn the wings cannot take off from a flat, because the rear tyre loses grip as the wings take the weight.
- Throttle no longer tucks the wings in the first 0.7 s off the ground, so leaving a ramp or runway with the throttle open does not fold them.

What one burn is worth, from a glide at 400 m and 72 km/h (12 s window, `handling.mjs`):

| How it is flown | Peak above start | Top speed |
|---|---|---|
| No burn | 0 m | 104 km/h |
| Wings spread, hands off | +94 m | 223 km/h |
| Wings spread, brake held (pull up) | +125 m | 210 km/h |
| Wings tucked during the burn, then spread | +65 m | 239 km/h |
| 2 s stoop, then spread, burn and pull up | +79 m | 260 km/h |
| 3 s stoop, then spread, burn and pull up | +44 m | 280 km/h |
| 3 s stoop, burn while still tucked | 0 m | 296 km/h |

So the rule a rider can learn: spread wings and nose up turn a burn into height; tucked wings turn it into speed. Air drag grows so fast above 200 km/h that a burn in a dive never buys more height than a burn in a glide, even with the ram effect. That was not the result hoped for (dive, then light it, as the best move) and it has been left as the physics gives it.

**Pans.** Where the megadune noise is lowest the dunes shrink to nothing, keeping their shape as they go, and leave hard flat ground.
- 11.7% of the field is dead flat (mean slope 0.27 degrees); another 13% is the fading edge.
- The longest straight run is 508 m. Flat out along it the bike reaches 145 km/h in 16 s.
- The ride now starts at the upwind end of that run, facing down it.
- Pans use the packed-sand look and carry no stones or scrub. Packed ground (pans and ramps) has 60% less rolling drag.
- The tallest megadune is still 236 m.
- Cost: megadune ramps longer than 150 m fell from 23 to 17, because six sat where the pans now are.

**Simulation after the changes.** Ramps: boost plus wings lands 17 of 17; throttle alone lands 9 of 17. Falcon stoops lose 12 to 99 m a cycle, unchanged. Stress run: no NaNs, 0.3 crashes a minute (unchanged), 10 stuck events in 45 minutes (was 5).

## 7n. Heavy landings, tail fin, flips, boost bar, wing glint (2026-11-16, Matthew's request)

**Landing.** Rebound damping is 2.4 times compression damping, so the spring's energy is not handed back. Impact speed (not air time) now sets `B.land`. On touchdown the bike loses up to 6% of its speed, the engine bogs (drive down to 40%, decaying over about half a second) and rolling drag is up to four times normal while the tyres are dug in. The view sinks on a spring and shakes, a skirt of heavy sand goes up with the dust, and the thump has a second, lower note and a chassis clunk. Drops at 5, 9 and 14 m/s give 1, 1 and 2 touchdowns. The hard-landing crash limit went from 19 to 26 m/s so a big jump landed wheels-first can be ridden out.

**Tail fin.** `Q` `E` (gamepad shoulder buttons). Without wings it yaws the bike flat and the heading is held for a moment after release. With wings it holds the nose up to 26 degrees off the flight path and a side force from the sideslip curves the path: 21 degrees in 5 seconds at 85 km/h, for 2 m more height lost than flying straight. Wing trim moved to `Z` `X`.

**Flips.** Wings away, more than 4 to 7 m above the ground, throttle and brake released once since take-off: throttle or brake then commands 5.5 rad/s of rotation. Hands off, the rider levels the bike the short way round. Once the bike is well over, that authority stays whatever the height. 500 points a flip. In simulation a 23 m jump takes a single or a double backflip or a front flip and lands; throttle held all the way down crashes; throttle pinned from the ground does not flip.

**Boost bar.** 3 s of burn, 15 s to refill, anywhere, hold to burn. Thrust is 2000 N at rest plus 30 N per m/s up to 4000 N (was 2600 / 40 / 5200 for a 2 s burn), so a full bar carries a little more total push than the old burn. A full burn from a glide: +119 m hands off, +150 m pulling up. **Consequence:** because the bar refills in the air, burning every 15 s gains more height than gliding loses (+360 m over two minutes in simulation). Sustained flight is now possible. Refilling only on the ground, or slower in the air, would restore the limit.

**Wing glint.** In the bike shader, wing skin only: 9 mm facets each tilted its own way flash when they face halfway between sun and eye, twinkle, and shift with a slow flex of the skin; under them a wide sheen and a hot core that blooms. Fades out past 40 m.

## 7v. The stoop: dive like a peregrine, zoom back up higher (2026-11-18, Matthew's request)

Asked for: dive like a peregrine falcon, reach insane speeds, and use them for lift boosts.

This last part is not physics, it is what makes a stoop pay.
- Speed in the stoop charges `B.sb` (0 at 108 km/h, full at 252). After the wings spread, while the charge lasts: almost no parasitic drag, 65% less induced drag, the wings take 5 g instead of 3.2 and do not fold their tips, and the elevator answers three times as hard. The charge fades over about 16 s, and in a couple of seconds once the speed is below about 150 km/h. It is gone on touching the ground.
